 Nintendo's latest Zelda game, The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the
Kingdom, has sold 10 million copies in three days, making it the
fastest-selling Zelda game ever. It has become the biggest physical
video-game launch in the UK in 2023, with estimated gross sales of
over $600 million. The success of the game is attributed to the
popularity of the Switch console and the reinvention of the franchise
into an open-world experience.


 The UK government has announced a £1bn package of support for the
semiconductor industry, but critics have branded it as "insignificant"
compared to the US and EU's $50bn (£40bn) support. Labour shadow
culture minister Lucy Powell said the strategy showed "significantly
less ambition than our competitors". Peter Claydon, president of
Picocom, suggested tax breaks and more emphasis on education to help
supply skilled professionals. The strategy will focus on areas the UK
is particularly good at, such as semiconductor design, cutting-edge
"compound" semiconductors, and research.


 Scientists at the University of Kent are using a Light Gas Gun to
fire projectiles at 1.5km per second to test a special gel made of
talin protein, which has an extraordinary ability to absorb impacts.
This protein was developed by Professors Ben Goult and Jennifer
Hiscock in 2016, and has been revolutionized by the AI AlphaFold. RF
Diffusion, an AI based on DALL-E, is being used to design proteins
with specific characteristics. Goult and Hiscock are working on
scaling up production of their shock-absorbing protein gel, with the
potential to reduce the amount of heavy ceramic used in bulletproof
plates.
