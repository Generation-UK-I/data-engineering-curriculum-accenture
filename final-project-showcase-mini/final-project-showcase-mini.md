## Final Showcase Prep

### Mini Project - final demo

---

### Presentations - Overview

- 10 minutes max to present, followed by 5 minutes max for Q&A -- be aware of timings as you prepare and during your actual presentation
- Use your slides as scaffolding and illustration -- keep text content brief and concise on the slides
- Have a powerpoint style presentation available to show

---

### Presentation Parts - Project Purpose

The showcase is a safe and supportive space for everyone. Its purpose is to highlight what you’ve learned and the training journey you’ve been on over the past 3 weeks. Strive for a balance between fun and professionalism — this is key.

Most importantly, enjoy the experience! This marks the culmination of a journey you’ve all shared. It’s your time to shine and proudly present the amazing work you’ve accomplished.

---

### Presentation Parts - Project Purpose

Explain what you were asked to build. Some things to consider:

- Theme
- Requirements
- Initial response to project task

---

### Presentation Parts - How You Started

Explain and illustrate what you did before you started to code. Some things to consider:

- Diagrams, notes or pseudo code you used
- Other brainstorms
- Examples/images/screenshots can really help to illustrate

---

### Presentation Parts - Project Demo

#### Your Design

Provide a visual explanation of the design of your project. Some things to consider:

- Explain the data flow of the application you built, from data sources through to presenting options on the screen
- Explain your choice of data structures, eg. when did you opt to use dictionaries vs. lists
- Explain how you approached error handling
- How can you visualise decision making in the logical flow of the application
- Optional - explain your choices for data storage, eg. Text file, CSV or Database

---

### Presentation Parts - Project Demo

#### Live demo

Some things to consider when doing a live demo of your application:

- How will you make sure it goes smoothly? What prep can you do in advance?
- Do some test runs -- the day before and on the day of your presentation
- Avoid showing and explaining code at a low-level, provide a user-focused demo of the product

---

### Presentation Parts - Project Demo

#### Documentation

Consider presenting your project's documentation, for example show a README file screenshot:

- Introduction to the theme and context of the application
- Business requirements
- Instructions on how to run the code and interact with the command line app
- Tech stack

---

### Presentation Parts - Diagramming Tool

- https://app.diagrams.net/ is a useful (and free) tool for diagramming that provides various software icons

---

### Presentation Parts - What Didn't Work

Provide an explanation of problems/obstacles/challenges and things that didn't work. Some things to consider:

- How you attempted to overcome your obstacles
- What you wish you had spent less time doing
- What you abandoned
- Why certain things didn't work

---

### Presentation Parts - What Worked

An explanation of your wins/successes and learnings. Some things to consider:

- Why certain things worked
- If you had more time for the project, what would you add, remove, and/or change?

---

### Presentation Parts - Anything else?

Offer anything else that you know to be relevant to your project and/or process.

---

### Presentation Parts - Q&A

Prepare for questions from the audience, which will be made up of your colleagues and instructors. Some things to consider:

- Put yourself in the audience's shoes -- what questions would you want to ask?
- What questions do you think the audience will actually ask?
- Practice answering them in advance
- Have a few questions ready to ask yourselves if/when there is a lull during your Q&A -- this is another way to show that you've reflected on your experience
- Also think of questions for when your colleagues are presenting
- Max 5 minutes for Q&A

Note:

There may also be other Accenture colleagues attending - mention this if applicable.
