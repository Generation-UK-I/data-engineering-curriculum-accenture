## Daily Open and Close Documents

We ran a daily open and daily close session each day for 10-15 minutes each. This directory has an example of a document we used for a daily open and a daily close.

Our general principles for this were

- Daily Open
    - Quickly recap what was covered the day before (only if there was a taught module the day before)
    - Quickly look ahead to the day - include a brief schedule
    - If it's a Monday, also give a quick look ahead to the week as a whole
    - Cover any other situation admin as required - anything the group needs to complete etc

- Daily close
    - Again briefly recap what was covered that day
    - Schedule for the next day
    - Any relevant announcements - e.g optional sessions, changes etc
    - Progress through the entire course - 'tick modules off' as we covered them
    - Feedback - see information on mentimeter below
    - 'Things to remember' - a small mantra to run through every day to encourage the learners
    - 'Problem solving mantra' - a set of steps for learners to remember when they encounter a problem

### Mentimeter

We found mentimeter (mentimeter.com) to be an effective method of quickly getting feedback from learners at the end of every day - it is quick and easy for the most part, and feedback is anonymous so people can be comfortable being honest. We used the learner feedback to tweak the structure of the course on multiple occasions, decide on optional sessions etc.

Creating an account with mentimeter is free, though there is a premium membership level too. They key drawback of the free tier, is that each 'presentation' can only have one slide (which will represent a single feedback question). So you need to create multiple presentations each day - by the end of the course I had about 120 created in my free account :)

There is flexibility in the types of question you can create - e.g. you can create a multiple choice poll, or have a question where responders have a 'free text' field to enter.

We tended to aim for having four questions at the end of each day - three 'standard' questions, and one 'bonus' question which changed every day.

Our standard questions were:

- How are you feeling at the end of today? (multiple choice)
- How could today have gone better? (free text)
- Is there anything you would like us to recap? (free text)

Here are a few examples of the 'bonus' question which we used throughout the course. We generally tried to use this question to put learners in good spirits, or force a little bit of self-reflection.

- Give a shout-out to a fellow learner who has helped you with something (free text)
- What is one thing I have learnt this week? (free text)
- One thing I am going to focus on for this week...
- Something I learned from watching other people's demos...
- My favourite thing about working in a team has been...
- What is the thing I have achieved on the course that I am most proud of

When you create each presentation, you will be given a 6-8 digit 'code' - you can provide these codes to the learners, and they go to menti.com, enter the relevant code, and they can respond to the questions.

Once people had responded, we generally quickly read out the responses (remembering they are anonymous) and reflected or responded to them as required.
